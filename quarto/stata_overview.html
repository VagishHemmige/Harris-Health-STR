Which file does what

Author

Vagish Hemmige

The do-files here are the ones that produce the published analysis: the main do-file, the data-build steps it runs, and Do Final Analysis.do, which starts from the data set they make. Earlier versions, earlier analyses and scratch files are not included. The files themselves are in stata/ and on the next two pages.

How the pieces fit

File by file

File Dated What it does R translation
Do Main do file Nov 2015, analysis date Dec 2017 Sets the analysis date and runs Steps 0B-8 with a log R/main.R
Step 0B Current Regimen Nov 2015 Every regimen over time from fill dates, 576 manual corrections, Complera recode, twice-daily flags 1. Regimens
Step 1A Pharmacy Part 1 undated Cleans drug names, flags twice-daily and single-tablet products, one row per patient 1 and 4
Step 2 CD4, Step 3 VL Dec 2014 Lab results, one row per patient 2. Labs and baseline
Step 4 Demographic Dec 2014 Demographics and risk indicators 2
Step 4B Insurance, 4C Psych Dec 2017 Insurance and prior care; psychiatry visit dates (added for the revision) 2
Step 5 Merge and Expand Dec 2014 One row per patient per day 2008-2013, labs carried forward and back 3. Patient-days
Step 6 Tokenize Pharmacy Dec 2014 Splits the pharmacy file into about 147 files to get round Stata’s memory limit Not needed in R (4)
Step 7 Merge Pharmacy undated Current regimen and dispense days on each day 4. Daily regimen
Step 8 Data Preparation Jun 2014 Pills on hand, days covered, still in care, suppression: the “Data Analytic file” 5. Pills on hand
Do Final Analysis undated; uses the Dec 2017 data The published analysis. Three regimens; exclusions; propensity score and weights; outcomes; sensitivity analyses 6 to 9

The main do-file still contains lines for files not kept here: two earlier regimen steps (0A and 1B, already commented out because 0B replaced them) and four earlier analysis steps (9-12, quarterly outcomes and survival models that are not in the paper). A stop line after Step 8 kept them from running.

Final Analysis and the paper

Final Analysis.do matches the paper (Hemmige et al. 2018): the three regimens compared (Atripla against darunavir or atazanavir boosted with ritonavir, each with Truvada), insurance in the propensity model, and psychiatry visits and a 50 copies/mL threshold as sensitivity analyses. Main does not call it: it was run by hand a section at a time, so it would not run straight through. A deliberate stop line sits partway down, and it creates VirologicallySuppressed2 twice, which Stata refuses.

Things the code does that the paper does not say

Kept as they are in both the Stata and the R:

  • Patients with prior care elsewhere and patients with no insurance status are excluded.
  • Viral suppression is 400 copies/mL or less, not less than 400.
  • Suppression on the initial regimen and the proportion of days covered are calculated over all follow-up to the end of 2013, not the first year.
  • The 50 copies/mL sensitivity analysis counts suppression at any time, not the first year.

Slips in the code

  • The models meant to use the psychiatry-adjusted weights (IPW2) use IPW. The R uses IPW2 (step 9).
  • (CurrentDrug4CurrentDispense) is missing its ==30. It does no harm, because the value is always 0 or 30.

Privacy

The do-files here are copies with patient-level details removed. Nothing else was changed.

  • Manual corrections. Step 0B had 576 lines correcting individual patients’ regimens, 396 of them for a specific dispensing date or date range, and Step 1A five more. A study ID with an exact date is more than a de-identified data set may contain, so each block is replaced by a note saying how many lines were removed and what form they took.
  • Notes about individual patients. Comments naming a study ID are replaced by a one-line note.
  • Paths and names. A local folder path is replaced by "<your analysis folder>", and a person’s name in a comment by a description.
  • Stop lines. Lines that deliberately stopped a do-file partway are replaced by a comment.

References

Hemmige, Vagish, Charlene A Flash, Jacquelyn Carter, Thomas P Giordano, and Thomas Zerai. 2018. “Single Tablet HIV Regimens Facilitate Virologic Suppression and Retention in Care Among Treatment Naive Patients.” AIDS Care 30 (8): 1017–24. https://doi.org/10.1080/09540121.2018.1442554.
